# Fact-check 2: The Paris Peace Settlement, 1919–1923 (paris_final.md, merge additions)

Covers only claims added in the merge. Rows already verified in paris_factcheck.md are not redone (noted where a claim overlaps).

| # | Claim | Verdict | Correction / note | Source URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The armistice ended the war "before any Allied army reached Germany" | wrong (as worded) | Allied armies did reach German soil: Russia invaded East Prussia in August 1914 (Gumbinnen, Tannenberg), and France took Thann on 7 Aug 1914 and held the southern Alsace valleys (Saint-Amarin to Dannemarie, German territory since 1871) to the end of the war. What is true: at the armistice the German army stood on French and Belgian soil, the front was ~720 km from Berlin, and the Rhineland occupation came only after the armistice, which fed the stab-in-the-back myth. Fix: "ended the war with German armies still on French and Belgian soil, leaving the victors..." | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_11_November_1918 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gumbinnen ; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thann |
| 2 | Clemenceau gave up his claim to the Rhine for an Anglo-American guarantee | correct (already verified, row 20) | No change. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guarantee_(proposed) |
| 3 | Sèvres (1920) partitioned Turkey; nationalists overturned it by force; Lausanne (1923) gave better terms | correct, slightly imprecise | Sèvres partitioned the Ottoman Empire, including Anatolia itself (French, Italian and British zones, Greek-administered Smyrna, Armenia, a Kurdish referendum, internationalised Straits, Eastern Thrace to Greece). Never ratified; the Turkish War of Independence overturned it; Lausanne restored a large territory in Anatolia and Thrace. "Turkey" is acceptable shorthand; "partitioned the Ottoman Empire down to Anatolia" is more exact. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Sèvres |
| 4 | "Britain took Palestine and Iraq, France took Syria and Lebanon" (mandates) | correct (acceptable shorthand) | Iraq was a Class A League mandate administered by Britain until 3 Oct 1932; the mandate's obligations were carried out through the 1922 Anglo-Iraqi Treaty and a 1924 British undertaking to the League. The earlier note means the draft mandate *document* was not enacted, not that Iraq was not a mandate. Optional precision: add "at San Remo (1920)". | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Iraq ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMahon–Hussein_correspondence |
| 5 | British and French empires reached their greatest extent | correct | British Empire reached its greatest extent after Versailles (+1.8 m sq mi, 13 m subjects); French colonial empire peaked at ~13.5 m km² in the 1920s–30s. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_the_French_colonial_empire |
| 6 | Italy got less than promised and "called it a mutilated victory" | imprecise | Less than promised: correct (London 1915 promised much of Dalmatia; most went to the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes). But the phrase was D'Annunzio's (Corriere della Sera, 24 Oct 1918) and was taken up by nationalist, irredentist factions, not "Italy". Fix: "Italian nationalists called it a mutilated victory." | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilated_victory |
| 7 | The Arabs, promised independence (Hussein–McMahon), got mandates | correct, simplified | McMahon (24 Oct 1915) pledged to "recognize and support the independence of the Arabs" within limits, with exclusions (west of Damascus–Homs–Hama–Aleppo; French interests); Palestine's inclusion is disputed. San Remo 1920 then allotted mandates. Shorthand acceptable. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMahon–Hussein_correspondence |
| 8 | Self-determination refused to Germans of Austria (Anschluss banned) and the Sudetenland, and to Egyptians and Koreans | correct | Saint-Germain Art. 88 and Versailles Art. 80 barred union; German-Austria claimed German Bohemia/Sudetenland, assigned to Czechoslovakia; Tyrol/Salzburg voted 98–99% for union, still prohibited. Egypt's 1919 Revolution and Korea's March 1st Movement followed the conference's disregard of their claims. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_German-Austria ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilsonian_moment |
| 9 | Guarantee to France "died with" the Senate's rejection of Versailles | correct | Consistent with row 21 fix: the Senate never took up the guarantee treaty, and Britain's guarantee was conditional on US ratification. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guarantee_(proposed) ; https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv13/ch27 |
| 10 | Hitler sold the Anschluss and the Sudetenland as self-determination | correct | NSDAP 1920 program demanded union of all Germans "on the basis of the people's right to self-determination"; Hitler's Sept 1938 Nuremberg speech invoked "the inalienable right to self-determination" for the Sudeten Germans; Anschluss staged with a 99.7% (unfree) plebiscite. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Czechoslovakia |
| 11 | March on Rome 1922; Great Syrian Revolt 1925–1927; Munich Agreement 1938 | correct | 28–31 Oct 1922; Aug 1925–June 1927; 29–30 Sep 1938. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_on_Rome ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Syrian_Revolt ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Czechoslovakia |
| 12 | Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Israel sit largely within borders drawn for the mandates in 1920–1922 | imprecise (dates) | "Largely within mandate-era borders" holds, but several were fixed later: Palestine–Syria/Lebanon line approved 7 Mar 1923 (Paulet–Newcombe); Mosul to Iraq by League decision Dec 1925 and Frontier Treaty 1926; Ma'an–Aqaba annexed to Transjordan 1925; Syria lost Hatay to Turkey 1939; Israel's borders are the 1949 armistice lines inside mandate Palestine. Fix: "drawn for the mandates in 1920–1926" or "drawn under the mandates". | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulet–Newcombe_Agreement ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosul_question ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Transjordan ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatay_State |

## What must change
Rewrite the armistice sentence (Allied armies did reach Germany: East Prussia 1914, southern Alsace 1914–18; say "with German armies still on French and Belgian soil"), make "mutilated victory" the nationalists' phrase rather than Italy's, and widen the mandate-border dates to 1920–1926 (or drop the dates).

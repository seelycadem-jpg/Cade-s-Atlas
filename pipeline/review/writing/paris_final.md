# The Paris Peace Settlement, 1919–1923

## Overview
The Paris settlement ended three empires. Austria-Hungary and the Ottoman Empire were broken up into new states. Germany survived as a republic but lost its colonies, most of its army and 13 percent of its European territory. It was charged reparations. It remained the largest industrial economy in central Europe.

Britain, France and Japan gained the most. Britain and France took the Ottoman Arab provinces and most of Germany's African colonies as League mandates. Japan took Germany's northern Pacific islands and its holdings in Shandong.

The settlement left the central question of the war unresolved: Germany's place in Europe. It also had no means of enforcement. The US Senate rejected the treaty, and the League of Nations had no army. By the 1930s Britain and France would not enforce the terms alone.

## Why it happened
The armistice of November 1918 ended the war with German armies still on French and Belgian soil, leaving the victors to dictate terms to a country that did not believe it had lost. Neither Germany nor Soviet Russia had a seat. French Prime Minister Georges Clemenceau wanted Germany too weak to attack again; he gave up his claim to the Rhine for an Anglo-American guarantee and accepted US President Woodrow Wilson's League of Nations to get it.

## What it did
- Versailles (1919): Germany kept 87% of its European territory but lost a tenth of its people and every colony. Army capped at 100,000.
- Reparations (1921): 132 billion gold marks on paper, 50 billion in practice. Germany paid about 20 billion before payments ended in 1932.
- Trianon (1920): Hungary kept 28% of its land. 3.3 million Hungarians were left in neighbouring states.
- Sèvres (1920) partitioned Turkey. Turkish nationalists overturned it by force and won better terms at Lausanne (1923).
- Mandates: Britain took Palestine and Iraq, France took Syria and Lebanon.

## Winners and losers
Britain and France won the most: their empires reached their greatest extent. Poland, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia won statehood, along with minorities that resented them. Turkey was the one loser that fought its way to a better treaty. Italy got less than it had been promised, and its nationalists called it a mutilated victory. China lost Shandong to Japan. The Arabs, promised independence, got mandates.

## Why it held or failed
The peace lacked consent from the people it governed and a power willing to enforce it. Self-determination freed Poles and Czechs but was refused to the Germans of Austria and the Sudetenland, to Hungarians, Egyptians, Koreans and Arabs, which handed every loser a principled case against the settlement. Germany survived largely intact, facing weak new states where Austria-Hungary and Russia had stood, so the terms held only while Britain and France enforced them. The US Senate rejected Versailles in March 1920 and the guarantee to France died with it. By the 1930s British opinion thought German grievances fair, and Hitler sold the Anschluss and the Sudetenland as self-determination.

## What it set up
- May Fourth Movement (1919)
- March on Rome (1922)
- Great Syrian Revolt (1925–1927)
- Munich Agreement (1938)

## Today
Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Jordan and Israel sit largely within borders drawn under the mandates.

## The detail
On 11 April 1919 Japan's proposal to write racial equality into the League Covenant won 11 of the 17 votes in commission. Wilson, in the chair, ruled that it needed unanimity, and the clause died.

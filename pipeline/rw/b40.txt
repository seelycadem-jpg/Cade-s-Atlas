  bluf: The Louisiana Purchase doubled the size of the United States and gave it the Mississippi and New Orleans, making it a continental power. It also reopened the question of slavery in new territories, which escalated over the next half-century into civil war.
  now: Fifteen of today's states lie partly or wholly in the territory, and the Mississippi basin it secured became the agricultural heart of the country.
  story: Napoleon needed money after his army was destroyed in Haiti, and gave up on an American empire. Jefferson's envoys, sent to buy New Orleans, came back with 828,000 square miles.
  why: Napoleon needed money for war with Britain and had given up on an American empire after his army was destroyed in Haiti. Jefferson wanted New Orleans, through which western farmers shipped their crops.
  then: It opened the West to settlement and set off decades of fights over whether new states would allow slavery. The Native nations who lived there were not consulted, and their lands were taken over the following century.
  fact: The US paid about 3 cents an acre. Napoleon sold it because his army had just been destroyed in Haiti.
## na180428 | 1804 | Lewis and Clark expedition | who: Meriwether Lewis, William Clark, Sacagawea | where: Missouri River to the Pacific
  bluf: Lewis and Clark mapped the new Louisiana territory and a route to the Pacific, strengthening US claims to the Oregon Country. The expedition previewed how American expansion would depend on Native knowledge and lead to Native dispossession.
  now: It set the pattern of the American West: exploration and trade followed by settlement, which dispossessed the Native nations whose help made the expedition possible.
  story: Jefferson sent Meriwether Lewis and William Clark to explore the new territory. Guided in part by Sacagawea, a Shoshone woman, the Corps of Discovery reached the Pacific in 1805 and returned with maps and scientific notes.
  why: Jefferson wanted scientific knowledge, a water route to the Pacific for the fur trade, and to stake a claim to the Northwest before Britain did.
  then: Their journey strengthened US claims to the Oregon Country, and fur traders and later settlers followed their route west.
  fact: Only one member died in over two years, probably of appendicitis.
## na180829 | 1808 | Atlantic slave imports banned | who: The US Congress; President Thomas Jefferson | where: United States
  bluf: The United States banned the importing of enslaved people at the first moment the Constitution allowed, but slavery itself kept growing. The internal slave trade from the Upper South to the cotton states became a huge business that tore families apart.
  now: The 'second middle passage' inside the US is far less known than the Atlantic one.
  story: The Constitution allowed a ban after 1808, and Congress passed one on the first possible day. Smuggling continued, and the domestic slave trade boomed instead.
  why: The Constitution barred a ban before 1808; by then even many slaveholders supported one, since it raised the value of the people they already enslaved and eased fears of revolt after Haiti.
  then: Around a million enslaved people were sold from the Upper South to the Deep South, often tearing families apart.
## na181230 | 1812 | War of 1812 | who: US vs. Britain and Native allies (Tecumseh) | where: Great Lakes, Washington DC, New Orleans
  bluf: The War of 1812 ended in stalemate between the United States and Britain, but it confirmed American independence, boosted nationalism and broke Native resistance in the Old Northwest. For Canada, repelling American invasions became part of its founding identity.
  now: It confirmed American independence and gave Canada a story of resisting the United States, one root of its distinct identity.
  story: Angry at British seizures of American sailors and trade limits, the US declared war and tried to invade Canada. Britain burned the White House in 1814, but failed to take Baltimore.
  why: Britain was seizing American sailors and blocking US trade with France, and many Americans blamed Britain for arming Native resistance and hoped to conquer Canada.
  then: The war ended in a draw, but Native resistance in the Midwest was crushed with Tecumseh's death.
  fact: The Battle of New Orleans was fought after the peace treaty had already been signed; the news hadn't crossed the Atlantic yet. The bombardment of Fort McHenry inspired 'The Star-Spangled Banner'.
## na181931 | 1819 | Florida acquired | who: John Quincy Adams; Spain | where: Florida
  bluf: Florida's acquisition showed a pattern of US expansion: military pressure first, then a purchase from a weakened power. The same treaty fixed a border to the Pacific, giving the United States its first legal claim to the Pacific coast.
  now: The deal shows America's habit of expanding first and negotiating later.
  story: Jackson's troops chased Seminole raiders into Spanish Florida and seized forts there. Weak and distracted, Spain sold the territory in the Adams-Onís Treaty and fixed a border with the US to the Pacific.
  why: Spain, weakened by the Napoleonic Wars and independence revolts, could not defend Florida, and Andrew Jackson's raids against the Seminole had already occupied much of it.
  then: Florida became a slave territory, and the US Army fought long wars to remove the Seminole, many of whom refused to leave.

Set D, with a fibre that is not a diamond

The diamond knot and the dot strand stay. The square goes, because maps use squares for forts and buildings and the globe will carry marks of its own. Triangles are out for the same reason: a triangle is a peak. The fibre is a shape of its own, not a diamond, a square, a triangle or a circle, since maps use all four. Four candidates, each shown in the four places. The globe pane carries a town ring, a fort square and a peak in grey, so you can see each fibre beside them.

1The filled hexagon

Six sides, solid. Reads as a mark of its own at every size and no map uses it for a place. Close in weight to the knot, so the eye tells them apart by shape alone.

2The open hexagon

The same shape with the ink taken out. Lighter than the knot and heavier than the strand, so weight and shape both say "middle".

3The bar

A short length of thread: a fibre, drawn as what it is. Quiet beside a title and on the globe; on the timeline it reads as a tick, which is what a timeline expects.

4The half disc

A half circle, flat side down, like a rising sun on an old chart. Distinct from the dot and the diamond, and it carries the idea of a part without being a diamond.

My recommendation: 2, the open hexagon.

It sits between the knot and the strand in weight as well as in shape, it is clear of every mark a map uses, and at 12 pixels it stays a hexagon. The bar is the quieter choice and the most literal; its weakness is that beside a title it can read as a dash in the text.

The six levels, from a country to an afternoon

Three frames hold the story and three sizes of event run through them. Every event has a date and a place. The link makes the size: a fibre is linked to a knot, a strand to a fibre or a knot. A figure, a quotation or a date on its own is a line inside a page, never a level.

The frames

Chronicle

The long story of one place or people, from its beginning to today.

Germany, from Rome's frontier to the Federal Republic.

Its page
A portrait of the place, then its stories in order. A two-minute read.
Made of
Stories, as many as the place has eras. Germany has twelve.
Readers see
The word chronicle.

Story

One era of the chronicle, with its own argument: why it went the way it did.

Weimar Germany, 1919 to 1933.

Its page
The briefing in a few analytic paragraphs, then its chapters, then Meanwhile, then Previous and Next story.
Made of
Chapters, as many as the era needs: five for Weimar, twenty or more for a long era. A story can belong to two chronicles: Divided Germany sits in Germany and in The Cold War.
Readers see
The word story.

Chapter

A section of the story built around its knot.

The Treaty, the first of Weimar's five chapters.

Its page
None of its own. It lives on the story page as one longer briefing paragraph (internally, the patch), then its knots, then a control that opens its fibres and strands.
Built on
Usually one knot, as many as the chapter needs. The Depression chapter holds two, the crash and the slump reaching Germany, with the graded link between them.
Readers see
The word chapter. Never "patch".

The events, in order of size

Knot

The chapter's major event. The chain breaks without it.

The Paris Peace Settlement, 1919 to 1923. Elsewhere: the United States enters the war.

Its page
A full event page: historical significance, lasting impact, what caused it, what it caused, the world before and after, Follow Historical Chain, the briefing.
Linked to
The knots before and after it on the chain, each arrow graded. Its fibres hang off it.
Mark
A diamond.

Fibre

A smaller event linked to a knot: its cause, a part of it, or its consequence. A step on the chain.

Versailles, 28 June 1919, a part of the settlement. The Senate rejects the treaty, November 1919, a consequence of it. Elsewhere: a battle of the war.

Its page
A shorter event page in the same shape, as long as the event needs.
Linked to
At least one knot, by a graded arrow. Its strands hang off it.
Mark
A filled dot.

Strand

A smaller event still, one step off the chain. It explains how or why. One vote, one signing, one day.

The racial-equality vote, 11 April 1919. Elsewhere: the Army issues trench shotguns.

Its page
A short page if its link is worth a page. Otherwise a line on the page of the fibre or knot it links to.
Linked to
At least one fibre or knot. A strand is the smallest event the atlas names.
Mark
An open ring.

Four rules that follow

Size belongs to the event and the chain together, never to the event alone.

The data records a size for each chain the event sits in. A page shows the size for the chain the reader came through.

No caps on count.

A chapter holds as many knots as it needs, a story as many chapters as its era needs. The count follows the chain, never a target or a limit.

Length follows the event, not the level.

A knot that is one day is short. A knot that holds five treaties is long. The level sets the role; the event sets the words.

The link makes the size, never containment.

A fibre need not happen inside its knot in time or place. The Senate voted in Washington four months after Versailles; the arrow from the settlement to the vote is what makes it a fibre. An event with no graded arrow stays a line, or a label on the map. An arrow is never added to justify a page.

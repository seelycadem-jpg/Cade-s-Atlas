Four levels, three sizes of event

A reader goes from the long view to one afternoon in four taps. Every event has one page and one size, and links decide where it sits.

The four levels

  1. Level 1

    Chronicle

    One long subject followed through time. It needs several stories.

    A place or people · Germany, The Jewish People A system or idea · Capitalism, Communism An age or contest · The Second World War, The Cold War
    • Named for its subject, never for where it ends.
    • It must show a line through history that no other chronicle shows.
  2. Level 2

    Story

    A part of a chronicle that one briefing covers, with one bottom line: what happened and why it matters.

    • A story can sit in several chronicles: one page, several parents.
    • Stories run in order of start date and may overlap.
  3. Level 3

    Chapter

    A stretch of a story in which one thing changes.

    • It holds any number of knots, including none.
    • Two changes make two chapters.
    • A link puts an event in a chapter. A shared date never does.
  4. Level 4

    Event

    Something that happened, with a date and a place. One event, one page, one size.

    • The size is knot, fibre or strand, rated once.
    • An event with no link stays a line on another page or a label on the map.

One event, many paths

The three sizes

Every event is rated once, by its impact on world history. It carries the same mark on every page.

Check one: did it last? The change outlasted the generation that lived through it.

Check two: did it cause the change, or mark it? An event drops only when a named earlier event had already decided the outcome. The printing press keeps its size; a surrender ceremony drops.

Reach is counted in people, never in countries. It counts lives changed in who ruled, how people worked, what they believed or whether they lived.

Reach travels only down the backbone. It follows the consequences that would not have happened without the event, and no further.

"Its world" is the connected world of its day. Before 1492 the Americas are a world of their own, so an event that reshaped them is a knot.

Inside a chapter

People

Links decide what a person gets. Fame never does.

  • A life

    A person whose own decision a knot depended on, or several fibres. A full page with Follow this life: the globe's year cursor slides from birth to death and turns to where the person was.

  • A card

    A person who acts in events, where no event depended on them personally. Role, dates, the events they appear in.

  • A name

    A person with no link into any chain. Role and name in the text only.

A life is its own page form and is never called a story. Dynasties are told inside stories.

Thinkers and their works

Burke, Hobbes, Confucius and Kautilya are in the atlas from the start. The thinker is a person. The work is an event, sized by the same three tests.

  • The page opens up the thinking with an idea map that adds to the text.
  • A link from a work to a later event needs evidence that the actors read it or invoked it.
  • An uncertain date shows as "~500 BCE (uncertain)". No date is invented.
  • These pages get a higher writing bar and their own agent loop.

Two guard rails against a Western lean

The audit, never a quota. For each era we compare a region's share of the knots with its share of the world's people then. A wide gap marks a place to research. We never promote an event to close a gap.

Chronicles planned by the same measure. Knots follow the chronicles we write, so the writing order is checked against the same shares.

A gap can be true. Some places and centuries left few sources and held few people. Once the research is done, a gap that remains stays. No place owes the atlas an event or a person for every year.

Against the mission

  • Holds

    History as cause and effect. Links decide chapter membership, so every page a reader opens sits on a chain.

  • Holds

    Why it mattered. Each story and chapter leads with a bottom line, and each event has a size with stated tests.

  • Holds

    Seamless for the curious. Four taps down, shared events across, Back at every step.

  • Holds

    The whole world. Reach counted in people, and the audit, check the lean toward Europe.

  • Holds

    What a chain turns on. Link grades mark each chapter's backbone, visible and filterable.

  • Holds

    The people who shaped the world. A life, a card or a name, decided by links.

  • Later

    The big questions. The ten themes are built after V1.0. Thinkers and their works are in from the start.

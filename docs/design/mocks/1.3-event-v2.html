Overview

The Paris settlement dissolved three empires: Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire and Germany's overseas empire. New states replaced them, from Poland and Czechoslovakia to Iraq and Syria. survived as a republic. It lost 13 percent of its European territory and most of its army, and it was charged reparations. It remained the largest industrial economy in central Europe.

Britain, France and Japan gained the most. Britain and France took the Ottoman Arab provinces and most of Germany's African colonies as League mandates. Japan took Germany's northern Pacific islands and its holdings in .

The settlement left the central question of the war unresolved: Germany's place in Europe. It also had no means of enforcement. The US Senate rejected the treaty, and the League of Nations had no army. By the 1930s Britain and France would not enforce the terms alone.

Causes and effects

Why it happened

  1. 1French Prime Minister wanted Germany too weak to attack again.
  2. 2British Prime Minister wanted a Germany that could still trade.
  3. 3US President wanted a League of Nations.
  4. TriggerThe armistice of November 1918 ended the fighting with German armies still in France and Belgium. Many Germans did not believe they had lost. The victors met in Paris in January 1919 without Germany or Soviet Russia. Clemenceau gave up his claim to the Rhine for an Anglo-American guarantee.

Why it held or failed

The settlement had two flaws. First, it applied self-determination selectively. Poles and Czechs got states. Germans in Austria and the Sudetenland, Hungarians, Egyptians, Koreans and Arabs did not. Every loser gained a principled case against the peace.

Second, it left Germany intact and surrounded by weak new states where Austria-Hungary and Russia had stood. The terms held only while Britain and France enforced them. By the 1930s British opinion considered German grievances fair. Hitler presented the Anschluss and the Sudeten claim as self-determination.

What it set up

Today

Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Jordan and Israel sit largely within borders drawn under the mandates.

The detail

On 11 April 1919 Japan's proposal to write racial equality into the League Covenant won 11 of the 17 votes in commission. Wilson, in the chair, ruled that it needed unanimity, and the clause died.

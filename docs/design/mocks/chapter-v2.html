The Treaty

·

The victors of the Great War wrote five treaties between 1919 and 1923 and dissolved three empires. Germany signed the first at Versailles on 28 June 1919 and survived as a republic and as the largest industrial economy in central Europe. No power took on the job of enforcing the terms.

The Briefing

The armistice of November 1918 ended the fighting with German armies still in France and Belgium, and many Germans did not believe they had lost. The victors met in Paris in January 1919 without Germany or Soviet Russia. French Prime Minister Georges Clemenceau wanted Germany too weak to attack again. British Prime Minister David Lloyd George wanted a Germany that could still trade. US President Woodrow Wilson wanted a League of Nations. Clemenceau gave up his claim to the Rhine for an Anglo-American guarantee.

The conference settled the terms before the German delegation saw them. On 11 April Wilson, chairing the League commission, ruled Japan's racial-equality clause lost after it won 11 of the 17 votes. The conference gave Germany's rights in Shandong to Japan, and on 4 May about 3,000 students marched in Beijing against the award. China's delegates withheld their signature.

The German delegation received the terms on 7 May. It signed the Treaty of Versailles on 28 June, after the army told the National Assembly it could not fight on. Germany lost 13 percent of its European territory and every colony, and kept an army of 100,000. France and Poland gained the land. The German right called the signature a stab in the back.

Four more treaties followed. Saint-Germain divided the Habsburg lands in September 1919 and banned Austria's union with Germany. Neuilly took Bulgaria's Aegean coast in November. Trianon took 72 percent of Hungary's territory in June 1920 and left 3.3 million Hungarians outside the new borders. Sèvres partitioned Anatolia and the Arab lands in August 1920. Turkish nationalists overturned the partition of Anatolia by force, and Lausanne replaced Sèvres in 1923.

The peace lost its strongest guarantor within a year. The US Senate rejected the treaty in November 1919 and again in March 1920, and the American guarantee to France lapsed with it. The League of Nations came into being in January 1920 with 42 members and without the United States. It had no army.

The terms still held wherever Britain and France chose to enforce them. In January 1923 French and Belgian troops occupied the Ruhr to take reparations in coal. But the settlement left Germany intact and surrounded by weak new states where Austria-Hungary and Russia had stood, and the Allies had set reparations at 132 billion gold marks in 1921. Germany paid about 20 billion.

The Treaty of Versailles to The May Fourth Movement

Contributing · high confidence

28 June 1919 to 4 May 1919

The Treaty of Versailles was one cause of The May Fourth Movement among several.

The claim

The treaty's award of Germany's Shandong concessions to Japan rather than China triggered the student protests of May 4, 1919 in Beijing.

The challenge

The New Culture movement was under way from 1915; the protest's size and legacy came from that, and Shandong was the occasion.

The verdict

Contributing, high confidence. The set: the New Culture movement (ranks first), the Shandong clause (second), the warlord government's weakness (third).

The Great War to The Treaty of Versailles

Necessary · high confidence · placeholder: the pilot graded this Sufficient, a grade since withdrawn

1914 to 28 June 1919

Without The Great War, The Treaty of Versailles does not happen.

The claim

The treaty was the war's settlement: its terms follow from the war's outcome, the armistice of November 1918 and the Allies' aims.

The challenge

The treaty's particular severity, the war-guilt clause and the reparations figure were choices made in Paris, chiefly by France, and could have been different.

The verdict

Sufficient, high confidence. Without the war there is no treaty; the war is enough to explain that there was one and that Germany lost, and the Paris conference explains what it said.

The Treaty of Versailles to German Hyperinflation

Contributing · high confidence

28 June 1919 to 1923

The Treaty of Versailles was one cause of German Hyperinflation among several.

The claim

Reparations set at 132 billion gold marks in 1921 added an external debt the Weimar state could only meet by printing.

The challenge

Germany had financed the war by borrowing rather than taxing, so the mark had already lost most of its value by 1919; the 1923 collapse followed the decision to pay striking Ruhr workers with new money after the French occupation.

The verdict

Contributing, high confidence. The set: war finance (necessary), the Ruhr occupation (ranks first in 1923), the printing decision (sufficient for the final phase), reparations (third). The popular chain overstates.

The Treaty of Versailles to Hitler Takes Power

Contributing · high confidence

28 June 1919 to 1933

The Treaty of Versailles was one cause of Hitler Takes Power among several.

The claim

Versailles handed every German party a grievance that the republic, as its signatory, could not disown, and the Nazis made revision their programme.

The challenge

Weimar survived the treaty for a decade and was stabilising by 1928; the Depression collapsed the centre, and the appointment of January 1933 was the conservatives' decision.

The verdict

Contributing, high confidence, for the appointment itself. The set: the Depression (necessary), the conservatives' co-option (ranks first among the contributors), Versailles (second), Bolshevik fear (third). The treaty's weight sits on two other arrows: it made the republic's legitimacy deficit (Necessary) and decided the revisionist content of German nationalism (Necessary). The Depression decided that Germany would break; Versailles decided what the break would look like.

The Treaty of Versailles to Germany Remilitarizes the Rhineland

Necessary · high confidence

28 June 1919 to 1936

Without The Treaty of Versailles, Germany Remilitarizes the Rhineland does not happen.

The claim

The remilitarisation of the Rhineland in March 1936 was a step in a programme whose content, the reversal of Versailles clause by clause, the treaty itself defined.

The challenge

Hitler's aims went far beyond revision, to Lebensraum in the east; the Versailles programme was the respectable cover for them.

The verdict

Necessary, high confidence. The specific sequence of 1935-39, conscription, the Rhineland, Austria, the Sudetenland, is the treaty read backwards; without it the programme has a different shape and a weaker domestic consensus.
